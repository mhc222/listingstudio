- 2026-08-29 (research, not yet built): "one residential-interior model handles exposure, WB, lens correction, highlight recovery" is the wrong architecture and the wrong sales claim. Three of those four are deterministic and solved — a diffusion model can only approximate them, and approximating lens/perspective correction is exactly how GEOMETRY_INTERIOR gets violated. Findings: (a) LENS — EXIF gives camera+lens, Lensfun holds the distortion/TCA/vignetting polynomials for that exact glass (same DB darktable uses); ~50ms, $0, and nameable in copy. (b) WHITE BALANCE — the real-estate hard case is *mixed illuminant* (tungsten lamps + daylight window in one frame), which no global multiplier fixes; solved by Afifi/Brubaker/Brown WACV 2022 (render 5 WB presets, small net predicts per-pixel blend weights, github.com/mahmoudnafifi/mixedillWB). (c) EXPOSURE — global via sharp/libvips linear-space gain + tone curve; spatially non-uniform via Retinexformer or HVI-CIDNet (CVPR'25, NTIRE'25 winner, open weights). (d) HIGHLIGHT RECOVERY — from a single clipped JPEG this is invention, not recovery; only honest from RAW or from a bracket set, which lib/hdr.ts already does. Architecture: deterministic "develop" stage in front of the generative stage (hdr.ts is the precedent — pure code, no ledger row), and only pay fal when a generative option is actually on (sky, grass, TV/fireplace, reflections, staging, declutter). Enhancement-only jobs drop from ~$0.021 + 5-8s to ~$0 + sub-second.
- 2026-08-29 (research): no purpose-built real-estate enhancement LoRA exists publicly — only interior *design* LoRAs (SedatAl/Interior-Flux-Lora) and generic realism/detail ones (flymy-ai/qwen-image-realism-lora, Civitai "Real Life LoRA Qwen"). Training our own is cheap and license-clean: fal's Qwen-Image-Edit trainer is $4/1000 steps ($0.40 minimum at 100), Qwen-Image-Edit is Apache 2.0 so weights and adapter are commercially ours, and CLAUDE.md already bars the FLUX dev route. Dataset = Matt's own BoxBrownie original→edited pairs (he owns them); 50-200 pairs is the normal style-LoRA range. Cost caveat: fal's LoRA endpoint bills $0.035/MP vs $0.021 flat for base qwen-image-edit — the LoRA buys quality/consistency, NOT savings. Savings come from the deterministic pre-pass.

- 2026-08-29 (research — lighting/shadow control, Matt: "this is key"): Relighting IS a solved specialist problem with released weights, but the useful move needs no training and no self-hosting. Landscape: curated list at github.com/tandaily/Awesome-Relighting. **IC-Light** (ICLR 2025) — V1 commercially licensed, V2 (Flux-based) non-commercial upstream BUT fal hosts `fal-ai/iclight-v2` with a "Commercial use" badge at $0.10/MP; verify that badge with fal before it becomes load-bearing (same risk category as the FLUX-dev rule in CLAUDE.md). **LumiNet** (CVPR 2025, github.com/xyxingx/LumiNet) — indoor scene relighting by transferring lighting from a REFERENCE image, weights on HF, >20% over prior SOTA on MIT Multi-Illumination. **ScribbleLight** (CVPR 2025, github.com/chedgekorea/scriblit) — indoor relighting from scribbles, albedo-conditioned + ControlNet on normal maps, so it preserves colour/texture/geometry by construction; best fit for our GEOMETRY_INTERIOR constraint but needs a local pipeline. **RGB↔X** (SIGGRAPH 2024) — intrinsic decomposition (albedo/normal/roughness/irradiance), the physically correct route. Skip Neural Gaffer + DiLightNet: object-focused and the literature reports inaccurate shadows/reflections.
